1Antimicrobial vs antibacterial vs antibiotic

Antimicrobial

Umbrella term: any agent that fights microbes (bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa). Can be natural or synthetic.

Antibacterial

Subset that targets bacteria only. Includes fully synthetic drugs like sulfonamides and quinolones.

Antibiotic

Strictly, a substance produced by a microorganism that inhibits or destroys other microorganisms (e.g., penicillin from Penicillium).

Selecting an agent requires 6 things: the organism's identity, its susceptibility, the site of infection, patient factors (age, pregnancy, renal/liver disease), the agent's safety and efficacy, and the cost of therapy.

3Rational dosing: the three killing patterns

Concentration-dependent · Cmax/MIC · AF

Higher peak means more killing. Aminoglycosides, Fluoroquinolones. Give big, infrequent doses (once-daily aminoglycosides rely on the post-antibiotic effect).

Exposure-dependent · AUC/MIC · VAFATL

Total drug exposure over 24 h matters. Vancomycin, Azithromycin, FQs, Aminoglycosides, Tigecycline, Linezolid.

Time-dependent · T>MIC · BVMC

Longer time above MIC means more effect. B-lactams, Vancomycin, Macrolides, Clindamycin. Give frequent doses or slow/extended infusions.

MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration) is the lowest concentration needed to inhibit visible growth. It is the minimum dose requirement every regimen must clear.

4Three ways bacteria resist

① Altered target

Mutation of the drug's binding site.
e.g., altered DNA gyrase gives FQ resistance; PBP2a gives MRSA.

② ↓ Accumulation

↓ Permeability (fewer porins, so less drug gets in) or ↑ efflux (drug gets in but is pumped out).
e.g., Enterobacter porins, tetracycline efflux in Bacteroides.

③ Enzymatic inactivation

Enzymes destroy the drug.
e.g., penicillinase in N. gonorrhoeae; aminoglycoside-modifying enzymes.

5β-lactam family: core rings

All share the 4-membered β-lactam ring (pink), the part β-lactamases attack. The fused partner ring defines the class. Penicillins and cephalosporins show cross-sensitivity (allergy).

PBP3

Penicillin's target transpeptidase in the notes. Blocking it stops peptidoglycan cross-linking, so the bacterium bursts.

PBP2a (mecA gene)

A replacement PBP with very low β-lactam affinity. It takes over cross-linking when PBP3 is blocked. This is the basis of MRSA, which is why methicillin, oxacillin, and even carbapenems fail. Ceftaroline (5th gen) binds PBP2a.

10Carbapenems, aztreonam, vancomycin, polymyxins

Carbapenems

ertapenem, meropenem, imipenem (+ cilastatin to block renal dehydropeptidase-I).
DOC for ESBLs, serious mixed aerobic + anaerobic infections, and Enterobacter. Not active vs MRSA (PBP2a). Epileptogenic, especially imipenem.

Monobactam: aztreonam

G(−) aerobes and Pseudomonas only. Poor oral absorption (given IV/IM or inhaled). Usable in meningitis. Excreted in urine.

Vancomycin

Glycopeptide that binds the D-Ala-D-Ala terminus. Systemic MRSA activity (pneumonia, bacteremia, endocarditis). Oral form is DOC for pseudomembranous colitis (C. difficile). ADR: Red Man syndrome from rapid infusion (treat with slow infusion and an antihistamine such as diphenhydramine), and nephrotoxicity.

Polymyxins

Polymyxin B (topical), Polymyxin E / colistin (IV), daptomycin. Disrupt membranes (LPS). Reserved for MDR organisms; never monotherapy.

11Protein synthesis inhibitors: "Buy AT 30, CELL at 50"

Aminoglycosides · GTANKS

Gentamicin, Tobramycin, Amikacin, Neomycin, Kanamycin, Streptomycin. Enter via porin channels; aerobic G(−) bacilli. "-mycin" comes from Streptomyces, "-micin" from Micromonospora.
Nephro: Neomycin (most), Tobramycin, Gentamicin · Oto: Neomycin, Amikacin (broadest spectrum), Kanamycin (most) · Vestibulo: Streptomycin (most; first used in chemotherapy), Gentamicin.

Tetracyclines

Four fused rings; bacteriostatic; broad. Food, antacids, dairy, and Ca/Mg/Fe/Al form chelates that ↓ absorption (except doxycycline and minocycline). DOC for Rickettsiae; doxycycline for leptospirosis. Don't work on 3PM. ADR: tooth discoloration, growth retardation; expired TTC causes Fanconi-like syndrome.

Macrolides · CARE

Large lactone ring; community-acquired pneumonia. Clarithro (methylated erythro) for PUD / H. pylori. Azithro is the prototype azalide with t½ 40–68 h, given once daily for 3–5 days. Erythro was discovered by Dr. Abelardo Aguilar in Iloilo soil (Ilotycin); its estolate salt causes cholestatic jaundice.

Clindamycin

(Dalacin-C®) Covers non-spore-forming anaerobes (B. fragilis). DOC for anaerobic infection above the diaphragm (brain to lungs).

Chloramphenicol · Linezolid

Chloramphenicol: reversible 50S binder, broad; past mainstay for typhoid. ADR: dose-related anemia and gray baby syndrome (neonates can't glucuronidate). Linezolid is an oxazolidinone that binds 23S rRNA, a "last-resort drug".

14Antimycobacterials: RIPE first line, then second line

Mycobacteria are rod-shaped aerobic bacilli with mycolic acid walls that divide every 18–24 h, so therapy runs for months (DOTS). Latent TB is asymptomatic and not contagious; active TB is symptomatic and contagious.

*Moxifloxacin

A fluoroquinolone considered for second-line therapy. Per the notes it's a recent replacement for ethambutol in some regimens. It is not part of RIPE.

15Antiprotozoals: amoebiasis, malaria, trypanosomiasis

Amoebicides

Mixed (luminal + systemic): metronidazole (DOC), tinidazole (2nd-gen nitroimidazole; also giardiasis and trichomoniasis).
Luminal: iodoquinol, paromomycin (an aminoglycoside).
Systemic: chloroquine (liver abscess, combined with metronidazole; not luminal), dehydroemetine (IM).

Malaria

Chloroquine (4-aminoquinoline): DOC for treatment and prophylaxis; binds heme so it can't polymerize to hemozoin.
Primaquine (8-aminoquinoline): kills liver hypnozoites; the only agent that prevents vivax/ovale relapse.
Atovaquone-proguanil: travelers.
Mefloquine: MDR falciparum with an artemisinin.
Quinine: cinchona bark; causes cinchonism.
Artemisinin: sweet wormwood; endoperoxide bridge; first-line for MDR falciparum.

Trypanosomiasis

Pentamidine: T. b. gambiense; also PCP.
Suramin: early stage (no CNS) rhodesiense; IV.
Melarsoprol: arsenical for late CNS-stage rhodesiense; slow IV.
Eflornithine: ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor; late-stage gambiense.
Nifurtimox / benznidazole: T. cruzi (Chagas).
